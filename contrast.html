Compared with a regular collaborative editor

The same three sentences, the same five editors, the same edits at the same seconds — told twice. First the way an ordinary shared document handles them, then the way Writ does.

01 · today

A regular collaborative editor

One copy of the document. Everyone types into it at once, and an edit that arrives while someone else is mid-word simply lands on top.

0.0s

Nobody agreed to any of this. Fourteen changes, none reviewed, and the sentences no longer parse. The revert war is invisible in the text — you would have to read the history to learn that two people disagreed about human nature.

02 · the alternative

Writ

The same edits, each on its own branch. The document line runs left to right; the dashed line is review.

0.0s

Agent tidy-ups flow straight through and merge. Every change of meaning stops at the review line and waits for a named person: @plato approves the one about Forms, and @j-rousseau blocks the one about human nature. The disagreement is now a decision somebody made, attached to a name, instead of a keystroke that happened to land last.
